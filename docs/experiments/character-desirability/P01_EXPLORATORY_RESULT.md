# Character Desirability — P01 Exploratory Result

**Status:** EXPLORATORY ONLY  
**Sample:** n=1  
**Date:** 2026-09-16

This record captures issue-discovery evidence from one real participant. It must not be represented as validation, a directional sample, market demand, or a character ranking.

## Silhouette observations

- Stimulus 1 was interpreted as a dark/shadow-like creature and associated with Eevee/Pokémon.
- Stimulus 2 was interpreted as a larger/heavier dark/shadow-like creature.
- Stimulus 3 was not identified as a normal animal; participant inferred psychic/spiritual or object-control qualities.
- Stimulus 4 was read as a slender animal/creature oriented toward speed or an assassin-like role.

## Unaided recall / confusion

Participant explicitly grouped stimuli 1 and 2, describing stimulus 2 as potentially an evolution/larger form of stimulus 1.

Participant summarized the four approximately as normal/basic, defense, spiritual and speed directions.

## Neutral-render observations

- Participant selected stimulus 4 as the one they wanted to explore further, reasoning from perceived strength/damage potential.
- Stimulus 3 was perceived as most different because it did not look like a normal animal.
- Stimulus 1 again triggered a Pokémon association.
- Participant considered the set capable of belonging to one world because of some visual/form/color similarities.
- Desired interaction was described as playing with combinations and upgrading.

## Issue-discovery implications

- Scout/Guardian separation needs review; size/mass difference alone did not prevent evolution-like grouping.
- Scout familiarity/IP association requires further originality review and redesign consideration.
- Spirit provides a useful structurally non-animal reference for architecture experiments.
- Wild communicated speed/aggression fantasy, but one participant cannot establish desirability.
- Combination/upgrading becomes an interaction hypothesis only; it does not validate a game-first product.

## Evidence boundary

Do not duplicate this response as synthetic P02/P03 data. Additional human participants, if available later, should receive the same frozen pilot stimulus without exposure to P01 responses.
